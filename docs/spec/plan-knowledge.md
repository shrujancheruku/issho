# Plan knowledge

From [Where plan knowledge comes from](https://github.com/shrujancheruku/issho/issues/543), amended by [Building blocks](https://github.com/shrujancheruku/issho/issues/544), [Routes worth walking](https://github.com/shrujancheruku/issho/issues/546), [Cities](https://github.com/shrujancheruku/issho/issues/555) and [Finding leads on a schedule](https://github.com/shrujancheruku/issho/issues/568). Research behind it: [Local recommendation sources](https://github.com/shrujancheruku/issho/issues/536) and [Terms and law on scraping and reuse](https://github.com/shrujancheruku/issho/issues/537).

## What Issho knows

Issho's knowledge grows only when a person **vouches**. It stores what has been vouched for (a piece), the facts recorded with it, the date, and a Google place ID.

There is no crawl and no catalogue of places. Issho keeps a **leads list** per city: places and activities nobody has vouched for, each held as our own note, its links and a place ID. A lead is never shown to a member, never used in a plan and never treated as knowledge. Everything else is looked up when it is needed and not kept, except a lead's own note and links.

## Sources

| Source | What it gives |
| --- | --- |
| An insider or the owner, in person | Vouches: the only way knowledge is added |
| Search for local recommendations, the city's food map, Michelin, press | Leads only |
| Suggestions from people with an account | Leads only |
| People who went | Corrections, and renewal of an existing vouch. Never a new vouch |
| Google | Identity (the place ID), the map pin, live hours shown beside ours, and the closed flag. It never picks or ranks |
| A model | Finds leads, when asked or on a city's cadence (the **lead search**), and sketches **outlines** from them; drafts plans from vouched knowledge; judges plans. It vouches for nothing and never names a place from memory |

No scraping of TikTok, Facebook or Zalo.

## Vouching

- **A visit in person is a full vouch. Each independent local source, cited, is half a vouch.** A piece needs a full vouch before a plan can use it, and an insider or the owner records it either way.
- **An activity, a meeting point and a stretch** take only a visit. Never from a desk, by the owner or anyone.
- **A food stop** takes a visit, or two independent local sources that agree. When it rests on sources, the first group's answers then confirm it or drop it.
- Whoever vouches records **cost per head, whether it takes a group of six, whether it needs booking, and real hours** including "until sold out", and picks the piece's area from the city's list ([Cities](cities.md)).
- For a bookable activity, the owner or an insider may ask the venue directly. The venue is never paid, charged or promised placement.
- A suggestion with a declared stake can be vouched, but only by someone other than the person who sent it.

What a vouch adds for a meeting point or a stretch is in [Routes](routes.md).

## People who went

- An attendee who answers "not as described" picks which stop was off, with an optional note. That is a correction on that piece ([Running Issho](running-issho.md#accuracy-reports) says when it makes the piece **doubted**), and a doubted piece is re-checked before its plans open again.
- New places from attendees go in as suggestions.
- **Feedback renews a vouch and never creates one.** An outing renews every piece in its plan, as of that day, when at least 3 attendees answered "as described?" and all said yes. That counts for both re-check clocks below, for every plan that uses those pieces. A "no" doesn't renew.
- A plan that keeps running keeps its pieces fresh; only pieces in idle plans need a visit.
- A plan shows, beside its record, "last confirmed by people who went on ⟨date⟩". The claim Issho makes is "kept up to date by the people who go", not "owned by the community": there are no public reviews or names.

## When sources disagree

1. A vouching visit, most recent first.
2. What a group reported. Two attendees agreeing overrides an older visit.
3. Local sources (the food map, press, the venue's own page).
4. Google.

Exception: Google is the authority on whether a place exists and where it is. Its "permanently closed" flag always triggers a re-check.

## Freshness

- Price and hours are re-checked every 6 months; the place itself (still good, still suits a group) every 12.
- A re-check is a visit, or an outing that renews as above.
- A re-check becomes a queue item when due; 30 days past due with nobody acting, the piece becomes doubted ([Running Issho](running-issho.md#the-queue)).
- A piece past its date fails the accuracy check, and its plan goes to its maker under the 14-day rule in [Plans](plans.md).

## What a city must supply before it opens

**12 plans that pass the bar, around 12 different vouched activities, across at least three areas, with at least 2 of the 12 in every place in the city's name**, covering weekday evenings and weekends. The Pilot is held to the same number. The rest of the opening list is in [Cities](cities.md#what-a-new-city-costs).

**No insiders are required.** The owner can do all of it. Because an activity and a meeting point need a visit, a city the owner can't visit needs at least one insider who can: a practical need, not a rule. With no insiders, the owner takes every insider duty: works all leads, fixes stale plans, redoes re-checks.

## The Pilot

The owner gathers the knowledge on foot before anyone is admitted, starting from search-found leads. This is also how one local person's recommendation, untested in the [prototype](plan-bar.md#what-the-prototype-showed), gets tested.

## What the terms research requires

Research, not legal advice ([Terms and law on scraping and reuse](https://github.com/shrujancheruku/issho/issues/537)).

- Keep only our own facts plus IDs. Google, review-site and social content is shown live with attribution and never copied, and nothing stored may be derived from Google content.
- With models, search and cite when needed; never build a catalogue. The lead search's tool must be one whose terms allow keeping our own notes and links, which rules out Google-grounded search.
- An area is never assigned from Google's coordinates, and Google's travel times are never stored.
