# Leads

From [Finding leads on a schedule](https://github.com/shrujancheruku/issho/issues/568), with the rules for suggestions from [Where plan knowledge comes from](https://github.com/shrujancheruku/issho/issues/543).

Visits are the bottleneck, not leads: an activity or a meeting point needs someone to go there, so a list that grows faster than people can visit is noise.

## The lead search

- The **lead search** is the search for leads a model does, run by hand or on a cadence. It only ever adds leads. Vouching does not change.
- **By hand** through the Pilot, and for any planned city until it opens. The owner or one of the city's insiders runs it from the leads list, with an optional "looking for…" line, at most once a day per city. Running it by hand stays available at every stage.
- **On a cadence** from the soft launch. Each city has a cadence the owner sets: off, monthly (the default) or weekly. It is a switch on the lead search, not a city setting.
- The owner can switch it off for any city at any time. A city with it off still gets leads from by-hand runs and from suggestions.
- It has one job. It does not watch existing pieces for trouble; Google's closed flag, the re-checks and what attendees report cover that.

## What it looks for

- **Activities first**: every plan needs its own vouched activity, and they are the hard part.
- It is steered by the city's **gaps**: activity types with few plans, areas and places in the city's name that are short, times of week not covered, and plans that were retired. A person's "looking for…" line is added to these.
- Food stops only near an activity lead. Never meeting points or stretches, which only make sense on foot.

## Where it searches

- The city's lead sources first, then the open web.
- Never TikTok, Facebook or Zalo, anything behind a sign-in, or review sites' ratings.
- A site that produced 3 vouched leads is proposed to the owner and insiders as a new lead source. A source whose last 5 leads were all dismissed is shown for removal. A person always makes the change.
- The search never contacts a venue.
- It receives no personal data.

## What a lead holds

Only these are stored for a lead the search found:

- the place's or activity's name
- which piece type and which activity type it would be
- the area
- one or two lines in our own words on why it is worth a visit
- the links it was found through
- the date
- a Google place ID when one matches

Never the source's text, photos, ratings or reviewers' names, and nothing from Google beyond the ID. A lead names a business or activity as it presents itself in public and says nothing more about a person: no phone number, personal profile or home address.

## Outlines

- The search can propose a whole plan as an **outline**: one activity lead plus a sketch of what would go around it, using vouched pieces nearby where they exist and other leads where they don't.
- An outline is a to-do for the person who visits, not a plan. It cannot be opened in the plan editor or sent for review until every stop is a vouched piece.
- A lead never goes into a plan directly.

## The leads list

- One **leads list** per city, in the Making tab, seen by the owner and that city's insiders.
- A lead is untaken, taken or dismissed. A person can do three things to it:
  - **Take** it ("I'll visit"), so two people don't go.
  - **Dismiss** it with a reason.
  - **Vouch**, which makes it a piece.
- Leads are not items in the queue: nobody is waiting on one and there is no default to apply. The 08:00 digest says how many new leads arrived, once per run.

## Beside suggestions

- One list. Suggestions always show above the search's leads, those from attendees of outings rated 4 or more first. A search lead is marked "found by search".
- A suggestion and a search lead for the same place become one lead carrying both sources. The person who suggested it still gets their private notice if it is used.
- The search never reads suggestions or who sent them. Leads are matched by code on the place ID, so no personal data goes to a model.

## What stops noise

- A run adds at most 10 leads, counting an outline as one.
- A run is skipped while 30 search leads sit untaken. Suggestions do not count toward the 30 and are never capped.
- Every lead cites at least one source a person can open. A food stop cites two independent ones.
- Before a lead is added, code checks that it sits in one of the city's areas, that Google does not flag it closed (looked up live, not kept), and that it is not already a piece, a lead or a dismissed lead.

## When a lead goes

| Lead | Goes |
| --- | --- |
| A search lead nobody has taken | After 90 days |
| A taken lead neither vouched nor dismissed | Back to the list, untaken, after 30 days |
| A suggestion | After 12 months |

- Nobody is told when a lead expires, and a person is never told their suggestion was dismissed.
- A **dismissed** lead keeps its name, place ID, links and the reason, and the search skips it for good. Only a person's suggestion can bring it back, with the reason shown beside it.
- An **expired** lead may come back when a source the search has not cited before names it.

## Judging it

- One number per city on the scorecard: of the search leads vouched or dismissed in the last 3 months, the share that were vouched. Target: 1 in 5.
- The number counts only once 20 leads have been vouched or dismissed.
- Below target for 3 months, the owner changes the search's instructions or switches it off for the city.
