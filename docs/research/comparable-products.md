# Comparable products: small groups of strangers meeting in person

Research for [#535](https://github.com/shrujancheruku/issho/issues/535) on the v2 map [#532](https://github.com/shrujancheruku/issho/issues/532). Gathered 2026-11-12. Feeds "Who Issho is for and what it promises" and "The stages of launch".

**Question.** How do products that bring strangers together in small groups work, and what is publicly known about what succeeded and failed? For each: the core loop from sign-up to a second outing; group size; who makes the plans; how places and activities are chosen; pricing and who pays; how they open a new city; and public figures or first-hand accounts on retention, no-shows and why people stop.

**Sources and trust.** Each claim links its source. First-party pages (the product's site, help centre, app store listing, blog) are the strongest for *how it works*; press is used for numbers and founder quotes. A claim marked **(unverified)** comes only from a secondary write-up I could not trace to the owner, or from a page I could not load. Private individuals quoted in press are described, not named.

## The short answer

- **Two shapes work, and they differ in who makes the plan.**
  - **The product plans everything** (Timeleft, 222, Grouper, China's blind-box dinners): a personality quiz, then the product picks the people, the day and the venue; you just show up. Groups of about 4–6. This is what scaled fastest: Timeleft reports 200+ cities, about 150,000 participants a month and €18M ARR within about two years.
  - **Hosts make the plans** (Meetup, Pie): anyone, or paid creators, post events; the product supplies discovery and RSVPs. Groups are open-ended and often too big to meet anyone. Pie had to add quiz-matched sub-groups of six because its events became crowded, then pivoted again toward permanent communities.
- **The second outing is the weak spot everywhere.** Timeleft has no product mechanism to meet *the same people again* beyond keeping "connections" and inviting them to your next booking, where strangers fill the other seats. Its own lesson is that bonds form over *repeated* exposure. RealRoots charges up front for six or more weeks with the *same* group because of this. Pie's pivot to "Community Homes" is the same lesson from the other side.
- **Pricing is a subscription to be matched; the food is paid separately at the venue.** Timeleft's US app store tiers run $19.99–$62.99; 222 charges $22/month. Venues are partners, sometimes paying for foot traffic (222), never the main revenue line in any source found.
- **New cities open by ads, not by staff** (Timeleft: "nothing but ads", about one city a day for a year, then cut from about 325 to about 220). Host-led products open city by city and stayed in two or three for years (Pie).
- **No-shows are handled with friction and penalties, not trust.** Timeleft: one-tap confirmation from 48 hours before, cancel up to 4 hours before, a waitlist that backfills seats, and in some markets a no-show fee of 35% of a month's subscription, triggered when two tablemates report you. 222 vets members and refunds people it can't place. Free events in general lose about 28% of RSVPs (median, one event-platform dataset).
- **Why people stop (only anecdote; no company publishes churn):** not clicking with the group, awkwardness, the venue below expectations, doubt about paying to meet people, and many dinners with no lasting friendship.
- **In Vietnam:** Timeleft runs in Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City (since early 2025), not Da Nang or Hoi An. Da Nang has free, host-led English-language Meetup and language-exchange groups, with 8–10 people per listed event. No local product doing product-planned small-group outings was found.

## Product by product

### Timeleft (Paris, 2023; global)

- **Core loop.** Sign up and take a short personality quiz on "interests, social energy, and values" → buy a membership → choose format (dinner; in some cities coffee, drinks, walks, women-only, singles), date and neighbourhood → Timeleft builds the group from quiz answers, age range, language and booking date → before the event the app reveals the venue and "a little about your group"; the group chat opens 30 minutes before the start → dinner lasts about three hours → afterwards you rate the event, pick which people to keep as connections, and give up to five "Impressions" (traits) to people you met → you can message connections and invite them to your next booking, "where new people fill the other seats". ([FAQ](https://timeleft.com/faq))
- **Group size.** "A group of 4–6 people" ([Timeleft blog, Aug 2026](https://timeleft.com/blog/what-we-got-wrong-about-connection/)); five strangers per table, three hours ([founder profile](https://timfrin.substack.com/p/inside-timelefts-journey-to-connecting)). Drinks and walks groups "can be a bit bigger" ([FAQ](https://timeleft.com/faq)).
- **Who makes the plan.** Timeleft, entirely: it "books the venue and chooses the restaurant", guided by the budget you set (one to three dollar signs) and dietary needs ([FAQ](https://timeleft.com/faq)). Budget tiers are roughly ≤20, 20–40 and 40+ for the meal ([help centre, via search snippet](https://help.timeleft.com/hc/en-150/articles/16452259807388-How-do-budget-preferences-work-for-Timeleft-dinners)) **(unverified: page returned 403)**. Each city sets its own days and times ([FAQ](https://timeleft.com/faq)); in Vietnam the dinners were on Wednesday nights ([Vietcetera, Jul 2025](https://vietcetera.com/en/4-apps-to-make-friends-in-vietnam-and-what-the-locals-say-about-them)).
- **Pricing and who pays.** Quiz and browsing are free; booking needs an auto-renewing membership whose price depends on country and plan length; food and drinks are paid separately at the venue ([FAQ](https://timeleft.com/faq)). US App Store in-app purchases: $19.99, $46.99, $62.99; 4.6 stars from 9K ratings ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6466442949)). In Vietnam one dinner cost "around 400,000 VND" for one user ([Vietcetera](https://vietcetera.com/en/4-apps-to-make-friends-in-vietnam-and-what-the-locals-say-about-them)).
- **How it got here.** Four pivots: 2020 an app around shared dreams, 2021–22 activities and groups ("no traction"), then in May 2023 weekly dinners between strangers, tested in 15 days with a Typeform, a WhatsApp group and Stripe ([founder profile](https://timfrin.substack.com/p/inside-timelefts-journey-to-connecting); [GDI](https://www.globaldatinginsights.com/featured/gdi-podcast-maxime-barbier-ceo-and-founder-timeleft/?amp)).
- **Opening a city.** "Using nothing but ads", with no city managers or ambassadors; Barcelona (Jan 2024) opened with "zero team on the ground"; about one city a day for a year to roughly 325, then cut back to about 220. Target depth: 50+ weekly participants per city. Restaurants are "partners, not bottlenecks". ([founder profile](https://timfrin.substack.com/p/inside-timelefts-journey-to-connecting)) Users learned of it from Instagram ads ([Globe and Mail](https://arc-dev.theglobeandmail.com/canada/article-timeleft-app-dinner-strangers)). The profile says the over-expansion hurt retention.
- **Figures.** €20k MRR Dec 2023; €1M monthly revenue 16 months after launch; €18M ARR and 150k monthly participants by Aug 2025; about 100 staff; 15–20k reviews a week; average use rose from 1.4 to 2.2 experiences a month after adding bar meetups, women-only dinners and running groups ([founder profile](https://timfrin.substack.com/p/inside-timelefts-journey-to-connecting)). "1.5 million events" in three years ([blog, Aug 2026](https://timeleft.com/blog/what-we-got-wrong-about-connection/)).
- **No-shows.** Confirm with one tap from 48 hours before; cancel up to 4 hours before; move a booking until 13 hours before; full events have a waitlist that fills freed seats; Timeleft cancels an event when it "can't assemble a good table"; "I'll be late" button around the start ([FAQ](https://timeleft.com/faq)). The FAQ says there is no fee for missing an event, but help-centre articles describe a no-show fee of 35% of a one-month subscription, applied when at least two tablemates report you absent and only if a warning was shown at booking ([help centre, via search snippets](https://help.timeleft.com/hc/en-150/articles/25012703925148-What-Is-the-No-Show-Penalty-Fee)) **(unverified: page returned 403; likely market-specific)**.
- **What they say they got wrong.** Their August 2026 essay lists ten revised beliefs; the load-bearing ones for Issho: connection "requires deliberate design"; bonds build "across several interactions"; they now mix shared interests with differences and say "repeated exposure matters more"; "creating the right conditions matters more" than finding the right people ([blog](https://timeleft.com/blog/what-we-got-wrong-about-connection/)). No retention, repeat or no-show numbers are published.
- **Why people stop (anecdote).** In Vietnam: one user found a close group after five dinners and kept meeting outside Timeleft; another was disappointed by a modest restaurant ("expecting something closer to fine dining"); another doubted "the point of paying to go meet people" ([Vietcetera](https://vietcetera.com/en/4-apps-to-make-friends-in-vietnam-and-what-the-locals-say-about-them)). In the US, a user who tried Timeleft and two similar apps over two years formed no meaningful friendship; users complain of not clicking and of awkward or forced events ([Boston Globe, Oct 2026](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/10/26/business/paying-friends-apps/)).

### 222 (Los Angeles, 2021; US and London)

- **Core loop.** Long onboarding questionnaire (about 30 Myers-Briggs-style questions plus social habits, diet, ZIP code, and what matters in a match) → the app "sends invites to social experiences happening nearby" → you pay to confirm → the algorithm picks who attends each table, and refunds you if no compatible members were found → you're seated with your matches at a public venue; reviewers describe a move to a second location (bar, club) afterwards → the table is the start of meeting other members later. No profiles, no DMs, no swiping. ([TechCrunch, Nov 2022](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2440702); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/222/id6450612690))
- **Group size.** Not stated by 222; its homepage pitches "sauna & cold plunge with your 4 friends" ([222.place](https://www.222.place/)).
- **Who makes the plan.** 222: "the algorithm pairs strangers and plans the evening's itinerary"; venues are vetted partners expected to enforce 222's guidelines ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2440702)). Experiences have broadened from dinners to day trips and activities ([Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/10/26/business/paying-friends-apps/)).
- **Pricing and who pays.** Started at $2.22 per weekly dinner, with venues paying for foot traffic ([TechCrunch, 2022](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2440702)). Now $22 a month for matching and curation, with the event cost (meal, class) extra; one user paid about $170 for a mountain day trip ([Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/10/26/business/paying-friends-apps/)). App reviewers say a subscription replaced pay-per-event ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/222/id6450612690)).
- **Opening a city / scale.** Began as backyard dinners funded by a $1,000 university grant; 27 staff and 21 cities with "at least one experience a day"; revenue tripled last year and is on track to quadruple to "the high seven figures" ([Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/10/26/business/paying-friends-apps/)). How a city is opened is not public. App Store: 4.7 stars, 7.7K ratings ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/222/id6450612690)).
- **No-shows and safety.** Members are vetted; experiences are always public and in a group; a phone line serves as an emergency hotline; anyone reported is banned for life ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2440702); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/222/id6450612690)). No no-show policy or figures found.

### Pie (Chicago, 2024; US)

- **Core loop.** Host-led: creators post events (often not public, built on friends-of-friends), members RSVP. Started as a 150-question quiz matching people one-to-one, then moved to groups because "the way platonic relationships form is, they form in a group setting" (Andy Dunn). ([TechCrunch, Nov 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/11/19/irl-social-app-pie-is-coming-to-sf-to-make-you-less-lonely))
- **Who makes the plan.** Hosts, paid by Pie: a $1M Creator Fund paid $5–10 per RSVP; one organiser had a run rate of about $100,000 ([TechCrunch, Nov 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/11/19/irl-social-app-pie-is-coming-to-sf-to-make-you-less-lonely)).
- **Group size and the crowding problem.** Events grew crowded and attendees didn't know whom to talk to; Pie answered with "Sparked by Pie": an RSVP quiz that splits attendees into groups of six with a group chat before the event ("A solution creates a problem", Dunn) ([TechCrunch, Mar 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/04/andy-dunns-new-app-pie-uses-ai-to-help-you-make-friends)).
- **Pricing.** Free to members; Pie "has not yet worked out a sustainable business model" ([TechCrunch, Nov 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/11/19/irl-social-app-pie-is-coming-to-sf-to-make-you-less-lonely)). $24M raised.
- **Opening a city.** One city at a time: Chicago, then San Francisco; about 23,000 users in Oct 2024, 130,000 monthly actives in two cities by Mar 2025; rollout to more cities waited on monetisation ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/11/19/irl-social-app-pie-is-coming-to-sf-to-make-you-less-lonely); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/04/andy-dunns-new-app-pie-uses-ai-to-help-you-make-friends)).
- **Pivot (Aug 2026).** "Community Homes": a permanent home for each group with a feed, membership controls and event tools; events become "more a product of having a great community-building ecosystem" (co-founder). About 300,000 users; time in app rose from 2 to 10 minutes after an activity feed showing which connections attend which events. No retention figures. ([TechCrunch, Aug 2026](https://techcrunch.com/?p=3156057))

### Meetup (New York, 2002; global)

- **Core loop.** Join interest groups → RSVP to events the organiser posts → repeat attendance happens because the group recurs. Most events are free or low-cost ([Vietcetera](https://vietcetera.com/en/4-apps-to-make-friends-in-vietnam-and-what-the-locals-say-about-them)).
- **Who makes the plan.** Organisers, entirely. Group size is whatever the organiser allows.
- **Pricing and who pays.** Organisers pay a subscription (free "Starter" plan only for first-time organisers); members are free, with optional Meetup+; organisers can charge dues or event fees to offset costs ([help centre, via search snippets](https://help.meetup.com/hc/en-us/articles/39428334646541-Who-pays-for-a-Meetup-group)) **(unverified: page returned 403)**. Organiser prices rose in June 2024, the first change since 2019, to fund a backend rebuild ([Meetup blog](https://www.meetup.com/blog/new-organizer-pricing-key-improvements/)). In 2025 Meetup tested a $2/month organiser plan with every member paying $2 per RSVP ([Bernoff](https://bernoff.com/blog/to-meetup-if-youre-going-to-change-your-prices-be-clear-about-it); [The Events Calendar](https://theeventscalendar.com/blog/tips/meetups-changes-and-what-you-can-do-about-them/)) **(secondary; scope of rollout unknown)**.
- **Opening a city.** It doesn't: a city exists when someone starts a group. Meetup claims "10,000 cities, 193 countries" ([Meetup blog](https://www.meetup.com/blog/new-organizer-pricing-key-improvements/)).
- **Da Nang.** "International Friends Da Nang": 1,588 members, English as the common language, in-person meetups monthly or fortnightly plus online weeks, recent listings show 8–10 attendees, free ([Meetup](https://www.meetup.com/international-friends-da-nang/)). Other free language-exchange and board-game groups run weekly ([search results, Nov 2026](https://www.meetup.com/find/vn--da-nang/networking/)).
- **No-shows.** Meetup publishes none. A check-in dataset across event platforms puts the median no-show at about 28% for free events and 17% for paid, and about 37% for free events of 10–49 people ([PheedLoop](https://pheedloop.com/blog/event-data-lab-report-5)) **(not Meetup-specific)**.

### Closer matches found along the way

- **Grouper (New York, 2011–2016; shut down).** Two groups of three friends met for drinks at a reserved table; matched by algorithm plus a staff team from Facebook data; no names or photos beforehand; the first round of drinks was covered by Grouper's fee ([Wikipedia, citing NPR, NYT, HuffPost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grouper_social_club)). Spread to 20–25 US cities and London by 2013; in June 2014 it shut its international operations and laid off staff (at least 30 before); closed October 2016 ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/group-dating-startup-grouper-shutters-international-operations-fires-staff); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grouper_social_club)). No founder post-mortem found; the reason for closing is **unverified**. It needed you to bring two friends, so it was not for people without friends nearby.
- **RealRoots (US, YC; women only).** Short AI-coach interview → matched into a small local group → a meet-and-greet → if you like the group, pay for a six-week run of about seven facilitated experiences with the *same* people, plus a group chat ([YC launch](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/OCH-realroots-the-mobile-friendship-app-for-women); [review](https://relationshiptech.substack.com/p/real-roots-relationship-tech-tool)). About $290 for six weeks; 120 cities; two users quoted still meet their group after it ended ([Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/10/26/business/paying-friends-apps/)); one critical account describes a group that never met again **(unverified)** ([review](https://relationshiptech.substack.com/p/real-roots-relationship-tech-tool)).
- **Bumble BFF and Geneva.** Bumble relaunched BFF in Sept 2025 around groups and community, built on Geneva, which it had acquired and is shutting down; Geneva had made no revenue as of June 2025 ([TechCrunch, Sept 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/18/bumble-bffs-revamped-app-is-here-focusing-on-friend-groups-and-community-building)). In Vietnam BFF is only a mode inside the dating app, and users report people mixing up friend and dating modes ([Vietcetera](https://vietcetera.com/en/4-apps-to-make-friends-in-vietnam-and-what-the-locals-say-about-them)).
- **Hey! VINA (shut down).** Women's friend-finding app; its site reads "BYE! VINA it's been a wonderful 9 years" ([GRASS review, via search snippet](https://grass.camp/blog/best-apps-to-make-friends-2026)) **(secondary)**.
- **China's blind-box dinners (Shanghai; BlindGo, Fandada).** Tables of six; matched by language (BlindGo, with Mandarin and Shanghainese tables) or by lifestyle and personality (Fandada); about 40 yuan to sign up, the meal paid separately (about 150 yuan); staff don't attend; each diner gets an icebreaker task; diners are mostly 32–37, older than founders expected; some groups keep their chat going afterwards ([Sixth Tone](https://www.sixthtone.com/news/1017835)).
- **South-East Asia.** Timeleft is listed in Hanoi, Ho Chi Minh City, Bangkok, Bali, Jakarta, Kuala Lumpur, Makati, Taguig and Singapore, not Da Nang or Hoi An ([Timeleft locations](https://timeleft.com/locations/)); it launched in Vietnam in early 2025 with a local coordinator ([Vietcetera](https://vietcetera.com/en/4-apps-to-make-friends-in-vietnam-and-what-the-locals-say-about-them)) and in Kuala Lumpur in July 2024 ([Free Malaysia Today](https://www.freemalaysiatoday.com/category/leisure/2024/08/08/heres-to-chilling-while-dining-with-total-strangers)). Smaller Singapore efforts (Socially, Dinner With Strangers) appear in press but could not be verified ([SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/week-asia/economics/article/3249123/more-singapore-companies-set-help-reserved-young-locals-break-ice-make-new-friends), 403). TripBFF matches travellers into trip groups ([Vietcetera](https://vietcetera.com/en/4-apps-to-make-friends-in-vietnam-and-what-the-locals-say-about-them)). No Vietnamese-built product doing product-planned small-group outings was found.

## Comparison

| | Who plans | Group | Same people again? | Price (who pays) | New city |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Timeleft | Product (venue, day, people) | 4–6 | No; invite connections into a new table | Membership ~$20–63; food at venue | Ads only, ~1 city/day |
| 222 | Product (itinerary, people) | small table, not stated | Meet members later; no DMs | $22/month + event cost; venues pay for traffic (2022) | Not public |
| Pie | Paid hosts | Open; quiz-split into 6s | Via communities (2026 pivot) | Free; Pie paid hosts | One city at a time |
| Meetup | Organisers | Open | Yes, the group recurs | Organiser subscription; dues optional | Someone starts a group |
| RealRoots | Product + facilitator | small group | Yes, 6+ weeks, prepaid | ~$290 / 6 weeks | Not public |
| Grouper (dead) | Product + staff | 3+3 friends | No | Service fee covered first round | Fast; 25 cities by 2013, then cut |
| BlindGo / Fandada | Product | 6 | Chats continue | ~40 yuan + meal | Shanghai |

## Gaps

- No company publishes retention, repeat-attendance or no-show rates. The closest are Timeleft's 1.4 → 2.2 experiences a month and Pie's time-in-app.
- Timeleft's help-centre no-show fee could not be loaded directly and contradicts its FAQ; it is probably market-specific.
- 222's group size, no-show policy and city-opening method are not public.
- No founder explanation of Grouper's shutdown was found.
