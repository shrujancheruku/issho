# Research: local recommendation sources

Issue [#536](https://github.com/shrujancheruku/issho/issues/536), part of the v2 map [#532](https://github.com/shrujancheruku/issho/issues/532). Researched 2026-11-12. This branch is never merged. It feeds the tickets "A prototype of good plans in Da Nang–Hoi An" and "Where plan knowledge comes from". Terms of use are the next ticket's job and are only noted in passing.

**Question.** Where do people actually find places to eat and things to do, in Vietnam (Da Nang and Hoi An first), Japan and the United States? Can Issho build on those sources? What does Google alone get wrong or miss in Da Nang–Hoi An?

**Method.** I used the sources' own sites and developer docs, company press releases, published surveys and state or national newspapers, searching in Vietnamese, Japanese and English. I opened Foody pages directly to check how fresh they are. Facebook groups, Zalo groups and TikTok feeds can't be read without logging in, so nothing here measures them directly. Anything I couldn't confirm at the source is marked **unverified**.

## Answer in brief

- **In Vietnam, discovery happens on social platforms. Google Maps is where people check a place.** Facebook (about 76 million users), Zalo (80.2 million monthly users, though it's for messaging) and TikTok (70 million monthly users) are where Vietnamese people hear about places. The only survey of food discovery I found (Hanoi, ages 18–25) says TikTok word of mouth has the strongest effect on where young people eat. Restaurants advertise the same way: 59.7% use a Facebook page, 42% a TikTok channel and only 18.2% Google reviews. Google Maps is still the default map and holds most Vietnamese places, so it's the obvious **index**. It's a weak **signal** of what locals rate.
- **Foody, the old Vietnamese review site, is effectively frozen.** Its owner now runs it as a front end to ShopeeFood delivery. Every Da Nang and Hoi An page I opened had its last review in 2019–2021 and still showed pre-2025 addresses. It's useful for history (which places have lasted) and useless for freshness.
- **None of the places where locals actually recommend things can be read by a machine.** Meta closed the Groups API in April 2024. TikTok's Research API is only for non-commercial academics in the US, Europe and a few other places. Zalo's APIs serve a business's own Official Account. Foody, ShopeeFood and GrabFood offer no discovery API. To use local knowledge, Issho has to **get it from people** (trusted locals, plans people make, curation) rather than **read it from a feed**.
- **The city government itself curates.** The Da Nang Department of Tourism runs `foodtourdanang.vn`: about 400 venues picked against five published criteria, in Vietnamese and English. It sits alongside the Michelin Guide (Da Nang: 47 places in 2026) and the official tourism portal. These are vetted lists of known quality. They have no API, though, and they lean towards places that appeal to tourists. The food map doesn't cover Hoi An.
- **Japan is the opposite case.** One local platform (Tabelog: about 890,000 restaurants and 101.75 million monthly users) sits next to Google Maps, and Recruit's Hot Pepper Gourmet search API is free and official. Young people discover places on Instagram.
- **The US is the case where Google wins.** Google Search and Maps lead, Yelp's API is now paid ($229 a month and up), and TikTok launched its own restaurant guide ("Must Eat") on 2026-10-28, in three US metros only.
- **What Google gets wrong in Da Nang–Hoi An** (details at the end): addresses after the 2025 merger, events tied to the lunar calendar and the season, street and alley food with no listing or a stale one, reviews that lean towards foreign visitors and can be gamed, and opening hours Issho isn't allowed to store anyway.

## What Google alone gets wrong or misses in Da Nang–Hoi An

1. **Addresses after the 2025 merger.** On 2025-07-01 Quảng Nam merged into Da Nang, districts were abolished, and the old city of Hội An became the wards Hội An, Hội An Đông and Hội An Tây plus the island commune of Tân Hiệp ([VOV](https://vov.vn/xa-hoi/hoi-an-1-di-san-4-don-vi-hanh-chinh-cap-xa-va-bai-toan-khong-gian-phat-trien-moi-post1310791.vov), from search snippet; [Resolution 1659/NQ-UBTVQH15](https://hoatieu.vn/phap-luat/nghi-quyet-1659-nq-ubtvqh15-2025-233542)). In February 2026 Google Maps still showed the old ward names and had given no timeline, and the lag hit Grab, Be and ShopeeFood ([VTC News, 2026-02-02](https://vtcnews.vn/vi-sao-google-maps-cham-cap-nhat-dia-danh-hanh-chinh-viet-nam-ar1001124.html)). Later reports say a phased update to ward level has begun ([Phong Vũ](https://phongvu.vn/cong-nghe/google-maps-cap-nhat-ban-do-cap-xa-phuong/)). Apple Maps searches both old and new names ([Tuổi Trẻ, 2026-05-14](https://tuoitre.vn/apple-maps-ho-tro-tim-kiem-bang-ca-ten-dia-phuong-cu-va-moi-tai-viet-nam-20260514105840129.htm)), and Da Nang publishes its own old-to-new lookup map at `bando.danang.gov.vn` ([VnExpress](https://vnexpress.net/da-nang-ra-ban-do-so-moi-4908913.html)). Foody and even the city's own food map still use the old units. **For Issho:** match areas by coordinates and official boundaries, never by address strings. v1's rule already says areas are official local units.
2. **Events tied to the lunar calendar and the season.** Hoi An's Old Town lantern night falls on the 14th day of each lunar month: traffic stops and shops switch off their lights from about 17:00 to 22:00, and lanterns are floated on the Hoài River ([Vinpearl](https://vinpearl.com/vi/le-hoi-o-hoi-an-mon-qua-lon-tu-pho-co), a resort operator; [VietNamNet](https://vietnamnet.vn/en/hoi-an-lanterns-at-full-moon-festival-E164348.html)). Food Tour festivals, fireworks festivals and the October–November flood season change what's worth doing or even possible. Google has none of this. It comes from the city portals and local knowledge, and plans need a lunar-calendar and events layer.
3. **Small, alley and morning-only food.** The places locals send each other to are often tiny, unsigned or open only a few hours. A typical example is a bún bò stall "mostly serving locals, rarely tourists, open mornings to early afternoon", which sells out early ([Wanderlog](https://wanderlog.com/place/details/5333622)). Such places may be missing from Google or listed with wrong hours. Issho may not store hours anyway ([data/README.md](../../data/README.md), Google terms). Whether Google covers them is **unverified** by any measurement here.
4. **Reviews lean towards visitors and can be gamed.** In Hoi An's Old Town, the review volume comes largely from foreign tourists, so ratings reward places that serve visitors well (**unverified** by language counts). Review bombing at Vietnamese scale is real: Google removed over 30,000 one-star reviews from one Hanoi hotel in a single day in November 2025 ([Tuổi Trẻ](https://news.tuoitre.vn/google-removes-over-30000-one-star-reviews-of-hanoi-hotel-that-denied-guest-prepaid-room-103251112142118137.htm)). Travellers report being offered discounts for positive reviews in Vietnam ([forum](https://www.australianfrequentflyer.com.au/community/threads/article-the-blessing-curse-of-online-travel-reviews.123023/post-2989474), anecdotal).
5. **Stale or contradictory status.** A sampled Hoi An Old Town listing showed "Closed" next to normal posted hours while the owner said it was serving through a typhoon ([Google Business page](https://business.google.com/v/thirty3-eatery/03311722067476222874/f54f/_), undated). Aggregators built on top of Google (Airbnb Experiences places, Wanderlog) keep showing permanently closed Hoi An restaurants.
6. **What makes a place compelling.** Google gives a rating and a category. The signals Vietnamese people actually act on are social: a TikTok video, a friend in a Zalo chat, a Facebook group thread. None of these can be read by a machine (above).

## Implications for the two follow-up tickets

- **"A prototype of good plans in Da Nang–Hoi An":** use Google Places for *existence, location and the place ID* only. Get *quality* from people: one or two trusted locals checking a shortlist, together with the city's own curated lists (foodtourdanang.vn, Michelin, Danang FantastiCity events) as the vetted seed. Expect Hoi An to need more hand-curation than Da Nang, since the city food map and Michelin don't cover it.
- **"Where plan knowledge comes from":** the data that reflects what locals think is locked inside social platforms, so the realistic sources are (a) people Issho trusts, (b) plans people make that can be repeated, (c) official curated lists and event calendars, and (d) Google or open POI datasets as the base layer. Open datasets (Overture, Foursquare OS) are the only base layer Issho may store freely; checking their Vietnam coverage is worth a spike. Japan is the exception, where Hot Pepper's official API could add structured local data.
