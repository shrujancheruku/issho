# Research: communities to recruit from in Da Nang and Hoi An

Issue [#337](https://github.com/shrujancheruku/issho/issues/337), part of map [#334](https://github.com/shrujancheruku/issho/issues/334). Researched 2026-11-04 against main at `92f1ad3`. This branch is never merged; anything adopted moves into `docs/ROADMAP.md` (Launch gates) and the READMEs.

Starting point: a waitlist soft launch of the Da Nang–Hoi An pack (15 wards, Vietnamese and English), for people aged 18–40, run by one owner alone. Issho is **platonic and group-first**: no profile browsing, no 1:1 messages, photos only for confirmed attendees, a "here for dating" report option (ROADMAP §1). Every outing needs **enough people in the same pack who can make the same time**, so the soft launch needs a concentrated first cohort, not a thin spread.

Sources are public pages only: official sites of universities and clubs, public Meetup, Reclub, Luma and Toastmasters listings, the coworking trade magazine Deskmag, and state-run newspapers. Organisations are named; individuals are not. Sizes are what each page said on 2026-11-04 unless a date is given. Facebook groups and Zalo communities, where much of Da Nang's community life happens, couldn't be read or sized from public sources.

## Answer in brief

- **Biggest pool: university students in Da Nang.** The University of Da Nang (six member universities) trains "over 60,000 students" ([VKU, 2024](https://vku.udn.vn/?p=20544)) and Duy Tan University about 20,000 ([duytan.edu.vn](https://duytan.edu.vn/about), not confirmed on the page itself, see below). They're almost all in the 18–24 range, speak Vietnamese and many practise English, and a strong habit of English clubs and volunteering with foreigners already exists. The channel is official: student unions, faculty clubs and international offices, which means slow, formal approaches. **Hoi An has no comparable university**, so its pool is expats, long-stay nomads and local young adults.
- **Fastest pool: the English-speaking social scene in Da Nang**, which already meets in small groups around an activity: book club (627 members), improv and karaoke (479), philosophy (673), international friends (1,563), Toastmasters (three clubs), pickleball (2,087 on Reclub). These are on Meetup and similar public platforms, organisers can be messaged there, and the people self-select for "meet strangers around an activity". Sizes are platform member counts, which overstate how many are active or still in the city.
- **Best bridge between the two languages: language exchanges and student tour-guide clubs.** Weekly exchanges (BlaBla Language Exchange, a Japanese exchange on Meetup) and student clubs that guide foreign visitors for free (Danang Free Tour Hometown Club, Hoi An Free Tour) put Vietnamese students and foreigners at the same table already. That's the mixed-language group Issho's matching is built for.
- **Hoi An's anchor is its coworking community** (Hub Hoi An), plus whatever runs in Facebook groups, which couldn't be sized. Plans in Hoi An's three wards will depend on Da Nang's people when travel limits allow (ROADMAP decision 2026-10-29).
- **Fit:** nearly every community here is already platonic and group-based. Two cautions: some nomad networking events are business-first (fine, but people may come for leads, not friends), and some social groups are explicitly men-only or skewed (The Male Brotherhood, Asian-background meetups), which strains the gender-composition rules if one source dominates the cohort. Spread recruitment across sources.
- **Not recommended as a recruiting channel:** the Ho Chi Minh Communist Youth Union (Đoàn Thanh niên) runs huge volunteer drives (over 509,000 volunteer participations reported, [Báo Mới](https://baomoi.com/vneid-t40508594.epi)) but partnering is a formal, political relationship that a one-person foreign-run app shouldn't start during a soft launch. Ticket #335 (Vietnamese law) may change this view.

Under-18 first-years exist; the 18+ rule must hold at sign-up.

**Fit:** good to excellent. These people already go to small activity-based gatherings with strangers, which is the behaviour Issho needs. Organisers can be asked to mention the waitlist or co-host a first Issho plan (a book-café crawl, a pickleball morning). Running clubs and pickleball skew Vietnamese and are the best route to local 25–40s outside universities. Meetup member counts include people who have left the city; treat them as upper bounds.

Avoid leaning on **The Male Brotherhood: Da Nang** (89 members, men-only circles) or single-demographic groups as a main source: a cohort skewed one way makes the gender-composition rules leave people unmatched.

**Fit:** good for 25–40 and for reaching Hoi An, but two cautions. Networking events are about business; Issho should be pitched as "do something you'd enjoy with a few people", not as networking. A one-person app can reach these spaces cheaply: ask to post once in a members' group or to leave a card at the desk.

## Expat and newcomer groups

- **InterNations** lists 41,658 members in Vietnam, focused on Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City; no Da Nang figure ([InterNations](https://in-cdn.net/vietnam-expats)). Not a strong channel here.
- **Da Nang Expats** (Google Group) exists for questions and get-togethers ([groups.google.com](https://groups.google.com/g/da-nang-expats/about)); activity and size not checked.
- **Facebook groups** for expats in Da Nang and Hoi An are referred to by every guide but couldn't be read or sized here. They're likely the largest English-language channel, and posting rules are set by each group's admins.
- **Korean residents** are the largest single foreign community in reports: "more than 11,000 Koreans" per a 2022 real-estate article (secondary, **not verified**); about 700 in 2015 per a Korean diplomat quoted by [Báo Đà Nẵng](https://baodanang.vn/english/politics/201509/city-leader-hosts-south-korean-diplomat-2442494). A Korean Consulate General has been in Da Nang since 2020. Korean is not one of the pack's languages; Korean speakers would join in English or Vietnamese.
- **How many foreigners live in Da Nang** isn't published in any source found. The closest is about 76,000 foreigners registering temporary residence in the first half of 2022 (secondary article, includes short stays, **not verified**). Since the 2025 merger with Quảng Nam, city statistics also cover a much larger area.

## Suggested approach for a one-person soft launch

1. **Start with the groups that already meet weekly** and are open to anyone: The Workshop, book club, language exchanges, Toastmasters, PiDN. Message organisers through the public platform, offer a co-hosted first plan, and give a waitlist link with a code per source so the waitlist counter shows which channel works.
2. **One mixed-language channel in parallel:** a UFLS or UD English club, or a student guide club, so the first cohort isn't English-only. Approach through the university's international office with the safety material ready.
3. **Hoi An through Hub Hoi An** and the Hoi An Facebook groups, with plans timed for weekends when Da Nang people can travel.
4. **Keep the ratio in check:** watch age (students cluster at 18–22) and gender per source before unlocking an area.

## Open questions for the owner

- Is the first cohort **Vietnamese-led** (students, runners, local clubs) or **English-led** (expats, nomads, Meetup)? It changes which source to approach first and how good the Vietnamese text must be.
- Approach **universities** formally during the soft launch, or wait until the public launch, when Issho has terms, a privacy policy, blocking and a track record?
- Offer organisers anything (co-hosting credit, a crew for their regulars), or only ask them to share the waitlist?
- Accept short-stay nomads on the waitlist, or prioritise people who live in the city, given the return-attendance metric?
