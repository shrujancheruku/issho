# Vietnam: data localisation, local presence, impact assessments, operator status

Research note, 2026-11-07. Not legal advice. It answers what Vietnamese law says about Issho, an app with Vietnamese users, an operator outside Vietnam (an individual or a US LLC), and Supabase and the API hosted in Singapore. Take it to a Vietnamese lawyer before the waitlist soft launch.

## 1. Cybersecurity Law 2025: storing data in Vietnam and having a branch there

### What the law says (mirror)

- **Art. 25.3:** domestic and foreign enterprises that provide services on telecom networks, the internet or value-added cyberspace services in Vietnam, and that collect, exploit, analyse or process personal-information data, user-relationship data or data generated by users in Vietnam, "phải … lưu trữ dữ liệu này tại Việt Nam trong thời gian theo quy định của Chính phủ" (must store this data in Vietnam for the period the Government sets). The second paragraph reads: "Doanh nghiệp ngoài nước quy định tại khoản này phải đặt chi nhánh hoặc văn phòng đại diện tại Việt Nam" (foreign enterprises covered by this clause must set up a branch or representative office in Vietnam).
- **Art. 25.4:** the Government details clauses 2 and 3.
- **Art. 44.2:** the Cybersecurity Law 24/2018 and the Network Information Security Law 86/2015 cease to have effect on 2026-07-01.

On its face the law is unconditional, as the 2018 law was. The trigger lives in the decree.

### Decree 333/2026, Art. 19: the trigger for foreign enterprises (verified)

- **The trigger is not automatic.** There is no user-count or traffic threshold. Under **Art. 19.3(a)**, a foreign enterprise in a listed sector must store the data and open a branch or representative office **only when** both of these hold:
  - its service has been used to violate cybersecurity law, and the MPS (Ministry of Public Security) specialised cybersecurity force has notified it and asked in writing for cooperation, prevention, investigation or handling "sau 03 lần và tối đa 06 tháng" (after three requests and at most six months);
  - the enterprise then has no remedy, does not comply, complies only partly, or obstructs or disables the MPS's protection measures.

  This is the same shape as Decree 53/2022 Art. 26, with the force-majeure window cut from 30 to 25 working days (**Art. 19.3(b)**: notify within 3 working days, then 25 working days to remedy).
- **The listed sectors include "ứng dụng trực tuyến" (online applications).** That is new compared with Decree 53, and it plausibly covers Issho. The other sectors are telecom, data storage and sharing, domain names, e-commerce, online payment, payment intermediaries, ride-hailing, social networks, online games, and messaging, voice, video, email or chat.
- **Procedure (Art. 19.6):**
  - the Minister of Public Security issues a decision;
  - A05 (the MPS cybersecurity department) notifies the enterprise and supervises compliance;
  - the enterprise has **12 months from the decision** to comply.
- **Domestic enterprises (Art. 19.2):** "Doanh nghiệp trong nước lưu trữ dữ liệu quy định tại khoản 1 Điều này tại Việt Nam." For them the duty is unconditional.
- **Data covered (Art. 19.1):**
  - personal information of service users in Vietnam;
  - data that users in Vietnam generate: account name, service-use time, credit-card information, email address, the IP addresses of the latest login and logout, and the registered phone number linked to the account or data.

  The decree's list drops "user relationship data", although Law Art. 25.3 still names it.
- **Form of storage (Art. 19.5):** the enterprise chooses, provided the data can be retrieved promptly for the authorities and meets national information-security standards.
- **Art. 19.4:** the enterprise must coordinate with the MPS to update the list of data stored in Vietnam and publicly notify users when it starts collecting more covered data types.
- **Durations (Art. 20):**
  - data storage lasts from receipt of the request until the request ends, with a **minimum of 24 months**;
  - the branch or office must stay until the enterprise stops operating in Vietnam or stops offering the service there;
  - **system logs must be kept at least 12 months**.

### Is Decree 53/2022 replaced?

- **Verified:** Decree 333 **does not expressly repeal Decree 53/2022.** Its effectiveness article (Art. 30) only sets 2026-08-19. Its transitional article (Art. 31) mentions Decree 53 only for pending cybersecurity appraisal dossiers.
- **Unverified (our reading):** Decree 53 detailed the 2018 Cybersecurity Law, which lapsed on 2026-07-01. Under the general rule that a detailing document lapses with the law it details, plus Decree 333 now covering the same subject, Decree 53's Art. 26 is effectively superseded. A lawyer should confirm this.

### Duties that apply now, with no MPS request (verified, Decree 333 Art. 16 and Law Art. 25.2)

These matter more for Issho than localisation. They apply to "doanh nghiệp trong nước và doanh nghiệp nước ngoài" (domestic and foreign enterprises) providing services on the internet in Vietnam:

- **Art. 16.2(a)–(b), account verification:**
  - verify user information when a digital account is registered;
  - verify the account **by a Vietnamese mobile phone number**, or, if the user has none, by personal identification number or another lawful electronic-ID method.

  Email OTP alone does not satisfy this on its face.
- **Art. 16.2(d):** only verified accounts may post, share or use interactive features.
- **Art. 16.3:** provide user information to the MPS within **24 h** of a valid request (**3 h** in emergencies).
- **Art. 16.4:** take down unlawful content within **24 h** (**6 h** in emergencies).
- **Art. 16.6, system logs:** the log must at least cover the user account, login and logout times, IP address and source port at login and logout, and a log of processing of posted information. It must be retrievable for **at least 12 months**.
- **Tension with the PDPL:** PDPL Art. 29.2 bars social networks from requiring an image or video of an ID document to verify an account. A personal ID **number** is not an image, so there is no direct conflict.

### Fines (verified, Decree 330/2026)

Under Art. 7.1, the cybersecurity fines in Chapter II Sections 1–5 are the **individual** amounts, and an organisation pays double. The cap is 100m VND for an individual and 200m VND for an organisation (Art. 7.3).

| Provision | Act | Fine for an individual | Fine for an organisation (×2) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Art. 29.1(a) | not verifying user information at account registration | 30–50m VND | 60–100m VND |
| Art. 29.2(c) | not storing Art. 25.3 data in Vietnam "trong thời gian theo quy định của Chính phủ" | 50–70m VND | 100–140m VND |
| Art. 29.2(d) | foreign enterprise providing services in Vietnam **without a branch or representative office** | 50–70m VND | 100–140m VND |
| Art. 33.1(a)–(c) | not storing data under the Decree 333 rules; not carrying out the Minister's decision to store data or open a branch; not keeping system logs for the required time | 30–50m VND | 60–100m VND |

Art. 33 also carries extra penalties: expulsion for foreigners (Art. 33.2), and orders to store the data or open a branch, or to suspend service and connectivity in Vietnam (Art. 33.3).

- **Unverified (our reading):** Art. 29.2(d) penalises a missing branch without repeating Decree 333's MPS-decision condition. Read together with Decree 333 Art. 19.3, the duty, and so the violation, should arise only after a decision. This is a drafting inconsistency worth raising with counsel.
- **Who can be fined (Decree 330 Art. 2.2(đ)):** the decree covers foreign enterprises "cung cấp dịch vụ xuyên biên giới" (providing cross-border services).

### Does a sole individual count as an "enterprise"? (unverified)

- Law Art. 25.2–25.3 and Decree 333 Art. 16 and Art. 19 speak of **doanh nghiệp** (enterprises).
- A natural person with no company is arguably outside the literal wording of the localisation and branch duty. But Decree 330 fines individuals, and the MPS would likely treat an individual operator as a provider.
- Do not plan around this gap.

## 2. Does the PDPL or Decree 356 itself require local storage?

- **Verified:** neither text requires local storage. A search of the full PDPL and Decree 356 text for storage-in-Vietnam language found none.
- **The PDPL's model is assess and file.**
  - Art. 20.1 defines cross-border transfer to include (c): an organisation or individual in Vietnam **or abroad** that uses a platform outside Vietnam to process personal data collected in Vietnam. Issho, with data in Singapore, falls here.
  - Decree 356 Art. 17.1(a)–(c) repeats this, and adds storage on a foreign cloud provider's service.
- **The PDPL applies extraterritorially (Art. 1.2(c)):** it covers foreign organisations and individuals that directly take part in, or are involved in, processing the personal data of Vietnamese citizens.

## 3. Decree 356: the impact assessments (verified)

| | Cross-border transfer impact assessment (TIA) | Processing impact assessment (DPIA) |
|---|---|---|
| Legal basis | PDPL Art. 20; Decree 356 Art. 18 | PDPL Art. 21; Decree 356 Art. 19 |
| Who | anyone transferring personal data across the border as Art. 20.1 defines it | controller, or controller-processor (files); a processor keeps one by agreement |
| Report form | **Mẫu số 09** | **Mẫu số 10** |
| Cover notice | **Mẫu 01a** (organisation) / **01b** (individual) | **Mẫu 02a** (organisation) / **02b** (individual) |
| Dossier | report, plus a copy of the transfer contract between sender and recipient, plus the sender's data-protection policies and procedures | report, plus a copy of the processing contract or agreement, plus policies and procedures |
| Deadline | **within 60 days of the first transfer** | **within 60 days of starting processing** |
| Authority response | the authority assesses the dossier and returns a pass or fail result **within 15 days**; if incomplete, it asks for completion **within 30 days**, after which a penalty may follow | same |
| How often | once for the life of the operator, then updated (PDPL Art. 20.3 / 21.2) | same |
| Updates (Decree 356 Art. 20) | **every 6 months** from first filing when there is a new purpose or a new or changed controller, processor or third party; **within 10 days** on reorganisation or dissolution, a change of data-protection service provider, or new or changed business lines; filed on **Mẫu 03a/03b** | same |

### The report's contents

- **Mẫu 09 (TIA, Art. 18.3):**
  - parties and contact details, and the data-protection contact;
  - purpose, data types, transfer activities and a data-flow diagram;
  - consent, retention and deletion;
  - safeguards after transfer, and the recipient's system diagram;
  - onward transfer to third parties;
  - a self-assessment of compliance;
  - an assessment of the recipient's protection level and of the risks, harms and mitigations.
- **Mẫu 10 (DPIA, Art. 19.3)** covers similar ground for processing.

### Where to submit

- **Verified:** Decree 356 says "trực tuyến hoặc trực tiếp hoặc qua dịch vụ bưu chính" (online, in person or by post) to the **Cơ quan chuyên trách bảo vệ dữ liệu cá nhân, Bộ Công an** (the MPS personal-data protection authority).
- **Verified:** Decree 330 Art. 55.1(b) names that authority as the **Cục An ninh mạng và phòng, chống tội phạm sử dụng công nghệ cao (A05)**. The Decree 356 forms carry "Lưu: VT, A05".
- **Verified:** PDPL Art. 22.3 says updates are made on the **National Personal Data Protection Portal** (Cổng thông tin quốc gia về bảo vệ dữ liệu cá nhân) or at the authority.
- **Unverified:** the portal's URL is `baovedlcn.gov.vn` (2023 law-firm reports). [MPS Decision 778/QĐ-BCA-A05 (2026)](https://congan.lamdong.gov.vn/UpLoaded/Files/pv01.phapche/2026/13.%20QD778%20-%20A05.pdf), on the administrative procedures, reportedly routes online filing through the MPS public-service portal. Neither document was read.

### Fees

- **Verified:** neither the PDPL nor Decree 356 sets a filing fee. A search for "lệ phí" (fee) found none.
- **Unverified:** whether a separate fee circular exists. None was found.

### Exemptions

- **PDPL Art. 38.2–38.3 and Decree 356 Art. 41:**
  - small enterprises and startups may opt out of Art. 21 (the DPIA), Art. 22 and Art. 33.2 (the data-protection officer or department) for 5 years from 2026-01-01;
  - household businesses and micro enterprises are exempt outright;
  - neither relief applies to anyone who processes sensitive data, sells data-processing services, or has processed data on **100,000 or more data subjects** (cumulative).
- **This relief does not cover the TIA (Art. 20).** A US LLC's status as a "small enterprise" or "startup" under Vietnamese criteria is **unverified**. A natural person is not an enterprise, so the relief plausibly does not apply to one.
- **TIA exemptions (PDPL Art. 20.6, Decree 356 Art. 17.3)** include:
  - transfers by a data subject of their own data;
  - an organisation storing its own employees' data in the cloud;
  - journalism;
  - emergencies;
  - cross-border HR management;
  - contracts for transport, logistics, payments, hotels, visas and scholarships.

  None of them covers Issho's main flow.

### Fines (verified, Decree 330/2026, Chapter II Section 6)

The personal-data fines are the **organisation** amounts, and an individual pays half (Art. 7.1).

| Provision | Act | Fine for an organisation | Fine for an individual (½) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Art. 55.1 | not preparing or keeping a DPIA; not sending it to A05 within 60 days; not completing it on request; not making the 6-month or 10-day update | 20–30m VND | 10–15m VND |
| Art. 55.2 | falsifying the DPIA or refusing to fix it | 50–100m VND | 25–50m VND |
| Art. 56.1 | not preparing a TIA before or during transfer; not filing within 60 days; not completing within 30 days of a request; not updating; not keeping it ready for inspection; not notifying the recipient's data-protection contact; not controlling onward transfer | 30–50m VND | 15–25m VND |
| Art. 56.2 | no transfer contract; no consent or notice for the transfer; inadequate security; obstructing inspection; continuing after a stop order | 50–100m VND | 25–50m VND |
| Art. 56.3–56.4 | transferring without a TIA, hiding or misdeclaring the flows, **in a way that leads to a leak** | 1–5% of the previous year's **Vietnam** revenue, banded by 10k / 100k / 1m affected Vietnamese data subjects; with no Vietnam revenue, a flat 200m–3bn VND | half |

Other measures:

- **DPIA (Art. 55.3(b)):** an order to **stop processing** until the DPIA is filed and accepted.
- **TIA (Art. 56.6(b)):** an order to **stop the transfer** until the dossier is filed and confirmed, plus deletion at the recipient.
- **Limitation period:** 1 year.

### Can a natural person operating from abroad be the filer and controller?

- **Verified:**
  - the PDPL defines controller, processor and controller-processor as "cơ quan, tổ chức, **cá nhân**" (agency, organisation, **individual**) (Art. 2.7–2.9), and scope includes foreign individuals (Art. 1.2(c));
  - Decree 356 gives individual filer forms, **Mẫu 01b, 02b and 03b**, which ask for a passport number, address, phone and email. Form 01a, by contrast, asks for a Vietnamese registration certificate and tax code;
  - Decree 330 Art. 7.1 fines individuals at half the organisation rate.
- **Unverified:** whether A05 in practice accepts an online filing from a foreign individual with no Vietnamese ID (VNeID). The MPS public-service portal usually authenticates with a Vietnamese account. A filing by post is the fallback the decree allows.

## 4. Data protection officer and local representative (PDPL and Decree 356)

### The duty (verified)

- **PDPL Art. 33.2:** "Cơ quan, tổ chức có trách nhiệm chỉ định bộ phận, nhân sự đủ điều kiện năng lực bảo vệ dữ liệu cá nhân hoặc thuê tổ chức, cá nhân cung cấp dịch vụ bảo vệ dữ liệu cá nhân." An **agency or organisation** must appoint a qualified data-protection person or department, **or** hire an outside data-protection service (an individual or an organisation). Art. 33.3 leaves the conditions to the Government.
- **Scope:** the duty is worded for "cơ quan, tổ chức". Unlike the controller definition, it does not say "cá nhân" (individual).
  - **Our reading (unverified):** a natural-person controller has no DPO duty.
  - A US LLC is an organisation, so it does have the duty, unless the small-enterprise or startup relief in PDPL Art. 38.2 applies. That relief opts out of Art. 33.2 for 5 years, except for sensitive data or 100,000 or more data subjects. Whether a US LLC qualifies is unverified (see section 3).

### Conditions on the person (verified, Decree 356)

- **In-house DPO (Art. 13.1–13.2):** appointed by a formal written document setting out duties and powers. The person must have:
  - (a) a college degree ("cao đẳng") or higher;
  - (b) at least **2 years'** post-graduation experience in law, IT, cybersecurity, data security, risk management, compliance, HR or personnel;
  - (c) training ("đã được đào tạo, bồi dưỡng") in PDP law and skills.
  - Every member of a DPO department must meet the same conditions (Art. 13.3).
- **The organisation's duties:** it signs a confidentiality-responsibility agreement with the DPO (Art. 13.5) and trains the DPO (Art. 13.6).
- **Outsourced individual DPO (Art. 15.2):** college degree; **3 years'** experience; "đào tạo, bồi dưỡng chuyên sâu" (in-depth training).
- **Outsourced DPO organisation (Art. 16.1):** a company in technology or legal consulting, with at least 3 staff who meet the Art. 15.2 conditions and a track record in security or PDP services. The organisation hiring it must publish the DPO's details to data subjects (Art. 15.3, Art. 16.3).
- **Nationality and residence:** **neither text requires the DPO to be a Vietnamese national or resident in Vietnam.** Searches for nationality ("quốc tịch"), residence and "tại Việt Nam" wording found none in Arts. 13–16.
- **Certification:** **no state-issued DPO certificate or licence is required.** The only condition is "đã được đào tạo, bồi dưỡng" (has been trained). The decree names no accredited course or issuing body.
  - The only certificate in Decree 356 is the "Giấy chứng nhận đủ điều kiện kinh doanh dịch vụ xử lý dữ liệu cá nhân" (Arts. 24–25). That is for businesses **selling personal-data processing services**, not for DPOs.
- **Unverified:**
  - whether A05 accepts foreign degrees or foreign training as meeting Art. 13.2;
  - whether a non-Vietnamese-speaking DPO is acceptable in practice (the forms are in Vietnamese);
  - whether MPS-affiliated "PDP training" courses are treated as the expected route. Commentators mention them; not read.

### Fines (verified, Decree 330 Art. 57, organisation amounts)

| Provision | Act | Fine |
|---|---|---|
| Art. 57.1 | no confidentiality agreement with the DPO; no training; appointment document lacks duties and powers | warning, or 10–20m VND |
| Art. 57.2 | DPO lacks the degree or 2 years' experience; DPO untrained; **no formal appointment document at all** | 20–30m VND |

Remedies (Art. 57.3): replace the DPO with a qualified person, or issue the appointment document.

### Local representative for a foreign controller with no presence

- **Verified:** the PDPL and Decree 356 contain **no duty for a foreign controller to appoint a representative in Vietnam.** A search for representative ("đại diện") wording found only the forms' "người đại diện theo pháp luật" (the filer's own legal representative). Nothing says "đại diện tại Việt Nam" (representative in Vietnam). There is no GDPR Art. 27 equivalent.
- **Verified:** the DPO's contact details are a required field in the TIA and DPIA reports (Decree 356 Art. 18.3(b), Art. 19.3; Mẫu 09/10, item 8). So a contact point must exist, but it need not be in Vietnam.
- **Separate regime:** a local presence can still arise under the **Cybersecurity Law** (a branch or representative office, after an MPS decision, under Decree 333 Art. 19.3; see section 1). That is not a PDPL representative.

## 5. The operator on a tourist visa: immigration and labour

### Immigration law

- **Mirror:** Law 47/2014, Art. 8 defines **DL** as the visa "cấp cho người vào du lịch" (for people entering for tourism). Work has its own symbols: **DN** ("work with enterprises in Vietnam") and **LĐ** ("labour"). Law 51/2019 split these into DN1/DN2 and LĐ1/LĐ2. The split is from memory, not re-read.
- **Mirror:** Art. 44.2(b) says "Hoạt động tại Việt Nam phải phù hợp với mục đích nhập cảnh" (activities in Vietnam must fit the purpose of entry).
- **Mirror:** e-visas (EV) carry the same purpose rule. Breach can lead to the visa being revoked or cancelled.
- **Unverified:** the administrative-fine decree for immigration (formerly Decree 144/2021, reportedly replaced in 2025) and its amount for acting outside the entry purpose. Not read.

### Labour law (Decree 219/2025, verified)

Decree 219/2025 replaced the foreign-worker parts of **Decree 152/2020 as amended by 70/2023** on 2025-08-07 (Art. 35).

- **Art. 2.1:** a "foreign worker working in Vietnam" is a foreigner who enters to work in a job listed in Art. 3, under one of 12 arrangements. These include:
  - a labour contract;
  - an intra-company transfer;
  - a service contract;
  - "người chịu trách nhiệm thành lập hiện diện thương mại" (the person responsible for setting up a commercial presence);
  - being transferred from a foreign organisation;
  - being a member or owner of a Vietnamese LLC.
- **Art. 2.2:** the "employer" is a Vietnamese-registered enterprise, organisation, contractor or a licensed individual business.
- **Exemptions (Art. 7):** no work permit is needed for:
  - a manager, executive, expert or technical worker in Vietnam for **under 90 days in total per calendar year** (Art. 7.13(a));
  - the person responsible for setting up a commercial presence (Art. 7.10).

  Both still need a notice to the provincial authority at least 3 working days before starting (Art. 9.4). The decree frames that notice as the employer's step.
- **Our reading (unverified):** the regime presupposes a Vietnamese employer or counterpart. A founder working remotely for his own foreign app, with no Vietnamese employer, client or entity, fits none of the Art. 2.1 arrangements literally. Commentators call remote work for a foreign employer on a tourist or e-visa a **legal grey area**: not authorised, and rarely enforced ([Vietnam Launchpad](https://www.vietnamlaunchpad.com/guides/digital-nomads), [Migaku](https://migaku.com/blog/language-fun/vietnam-digital-nomad-visa-workarounds-legal-options-for-remote-workers)).

  The Pilot is not just remote coding. It includes running in-person outings with Vietnamese participants, meeting venues, and recruiting. That looks more like business activity in Vietnam than tourism, and strains Art. 44.2(b). A DN visa, or the commercial-presence route, is the conservative path. Ask a Vietnamese immigration lawyer.

### Does being physically in Vietnam change "provider from abroad"?

- **Mirror:** Decree 147/2024 Art. 3.33 defines cross-border information provision as "tổ chức, cá nhân **tại nước ngoài** cung cấp thông tin …" (an organisation or individual **located abroad**) to users in Vietnam.
- **Mirror:** Art. 23.3 notification duties apply only to foreign providers that either rent data storage in Vietnam, or have **≥ 100,000 total visits from Vietnam per month** (6-month average). They must then notify contacts to the ministry within 60 days. The ministry named is the former MIC, whose functions moved in 2025 to the Ministry of Science and Technology; that move is unverified.
- **Unverified (our reading):** with a US LLC as provider, the provider stays "abroad" whoever is physically present. With an individual operator living in Vietnam, the "tại nước ngoài" premise weakens, and the operator could be treated as a provider in Vietnam, subject to the domestic regime. Domestic social-network or general-website rules may then require licences or notification, and Decree 333 Art. 19.2 would apply the unconditional **domestic** localisation duty.
- **Cybersecurity Law:** it distinguishes "doanh nghiệp trong nước" from "doanh nghiệp nước ngoài/ngoài nước" (domestic and foreign enterprises) by the enterprise, not by where people sit. A US LLC stays foreign. An individual has no enterprise nationality, which is unresolved (see section 1).

## 6. App stores: organisation accounts (verified, first-party help pages)

### Google Play

- **Closed testing ([Play Console Help 14151465](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465)):** "a minimum of 12 testers who have been opted in continuously for at least 14 days". It applies to **personal developer accounts created after November 13, 2023**.
  - **Verified:** the page is scoped only to personal accounts and does not mention organisation accounts.
  - **Not stated:** that organisation accounts are expressly exempt. That is implied by the scope, not written.
- **Organisation account ([Play Console Help 13628312](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13628312)):**
  - a **D-U-N-S number** is required ("You will not be able to create a developer account for an organization without one"). It is free from D&B, and "This process can take up to 30 days so you should plan ahead";
  - the organisation's name, address, phone and website must match the linked Google Payments profile;
  - contact and developer emails and phones are verified by one-time password;
  - identity is verified before publishing;
  - payment-method verification (only if you monetise) takes up to 5 days.

### Apple Developer Program

Source: [developer.apple.com/programs/enroll](https://developer.apple.com/programs/enroll/).

- **Entity:** the enrolee must be "a legal entity that can enter into contracts with Apple. We do not accept DBAs, fictitious business names, trade names, or branches." A US LLC qualifies; a sole proprietor under a DBA does not.
- **D-U-N-S:** required (government entities excepted).
- **Signer:** the enrolling person must have legal authority to bind the organisation.
- **Domain and website:** a work email on the organisation's domain, and a public, functional website on that domain. Social pages and placeholders are rejected.
- **Account:** an Apple Account with two-factor authentication, under legal names.
- **Fee:** 99 USD per year.
- **Lead time ([Apple D-U-N-S help](https://developer.apple.com/help/account/membership/D-U-N-S/)):** "allow up to 5 business days to receive your number from D&B", then "up to 2 business days for Apple to receive your information". Contact D&B if it passes two weeks.
- **Unverified:** how long Apple then takes to review an organisation enrolment (it varies).

## What this means for Issho (our reading, unverified)

1. **Pilot, data in Singapore:**
   - localisation and a branch are **not required up front** for a foreign provider. They follow only an MPS decision after repeated failure to cooperate, with 12 months to comply;
   - **TIA and DPIA filing is required within 60 days** of first processing or transfer, unless an exemption applies. Only the small-enterprise relief could apply, and it covers the DPIA only;
   - Decree 333 Art. 16 **phone-number account verification**, the 24 h response and takedown duties, and 12-month logs **apply now**.
2. **The operator's form matters.** A US LLC keeps the "foreign provider" framing, and is needed anyway for Apple and Google organisation accounts. An individual living in Vietnam risks the domestic regime and immigration-purpose issues.
3. **Open questions for counsel:**
   - Art. 29.2(d) versus Decree 333 Art. 19.3;
   - whether a natural person counts as a "doanh nghiệp";
   - whether email OTP plus Vietnamese-phone verification is required at the Pilot's size;
   - the A05 online filing route for foreign filers;
   - whether a US LLC qualifies for the small-enterprise relief from the DPO duty (otherwise it must appoint a trained DPO with a degree and 2 years' experience, or hire a qualifying outside DPO service, not necessarily based in Vietnam);
   - whether hosting operations in person on a DL or EV visa is permitted.
