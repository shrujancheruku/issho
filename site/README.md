# Marketing site

Static landing page for Issho at joinissho.com, published to Cloudflare by `.github/workflows/deploy-web.yml` on every push to `main` that touches `site/`.

- `index.html`: single self-contained page (inline CSS, no build step).
- `logo.svg`, `favicon.svg` (the round blue mark), `apple-touch-icon.png` and `og-image.png` (the link preview, 1200×630) are generated from `assets/brand/` by `scripts/brand/build_variants.py` (`make app-icons`). Don't edit them by hand.
- Light and dark, following the system setting like the app, with a switch in the header (in place of a second waitlist link) that overrides it and is remembered in `localStorage`. A tiny script in `<head>` applies the remembered choice before the first paint, so the page never flashes the other theme. Switching cross-fades the colours (the `theming` class turns on colour transitions for half a second; instant with reduced motion). Without JavaScript the switch stays hidden and the page follows the system. The themes are the app's two canvases ([docs/design-system.md](../docs/design-system.md)), Ai night with the Kogane gold `#DEA92C` and its tint `#F2E2B2` for the big headings, and the light canvas `#F3F5F9` with ink headings and deep gold `#885716` for gold text. Gold fills the thread and the button in both. The three interest chips are filled as in the app (vermilion "Love it", gold "Like it", pale gold "Open to try"); shu red `#DA4528` is only the 日 sun. Headings use Dela Gothic One; body and Japanese use Zen Kaku Gothic New (both Google Fonts).
- The email/city form is a placeholder: it prevents submission and sends nothing. Wire it to a backend before collecting real data, and update the privacy wording at the same time.

Preview locally: `python3 -m http.server -d site 8000`.

## Design notes

- The page tells one outing from start to finish: a plan, date set, going, on the day, how was it, then a crew. A gold thread fades in just below the header logo and runs down the left, with a knot at each stage, the crew's last. The knot the thread has most recently reached breathes (a gold ring easing out every 2.4 s).
- A small script at the end of the page draws the thread as you scroll (the fill ends at the middle of the screen, and each knot turns gold when the fill reaches it). It uses scroll events, not CSS scroll-driven animations, so it works in every browser. Without JavaScript, or with reduced motion, the whole thread is drawn. The fill ends at the bottom of `.story`, which is why the crew stage sits just after it.
- Motion uses the app's tokens (`apps/mobile/src/constants/motion.ts`, [docs/design-system.md](../docs/design-system.md#motion)). Each stage's fragment plays once, the first time the thread reaches it (the script adds `.reached`): the knot ties with a small spring; the plan's route draws itself over 1.6 s with the stops popping in, then a gold walker strolls the map's route (5.2 s, resting 1.8 s); the date slot turns gold; the group gathers and "I can go" becomes "You are going." as the count rolls from 4 to 5; on the day, the group chat (styled like the app's, with Maya's message already in it) types and sends your follow-up, then shows Kenji typing; the picks tick one by one; the crew offer (after the app's, an indigo head with the four of you on a thread) goes from one yes to Maya's, then yours, the thread fills, it starts and everyone hops. These two scenes start once their card is on screen, and the markup holds their end state. Buttons and the switch scale to 0.97 when pressed. The walker and the typing dots, the only loops, run only while their fragment is on screen (`.live`). With reduced motion or without JavaScript, every fragment shows its finished state.
- The plan card has a map of the walk, drawn as inline SVG from OpenStreetMap by `scripts/site/build_map.py` (between the `map:start` and `map:end` markers; don't edit it by hand). The walk is routed along real paths: from Kichijoji Station's south (park) exit down Suehiro-dori and Benten-dori to the east end of Inokashira Pond, once around the pond (west along the south shore, round the western arm, back along the north shore) to Nanai Bridge, then up Nanaibashi-dori to the café, about 2 km, so the map and the stops agree with the place. The map takes the page's light and dark colours (`--map-*`), the route draws itself as the thread reaches the plan, and the gold walker strolls it while the card is on screen. The figure credits OpenStreetMap (ODbL). Rerun the script to refresh the map or change the walk.
- Each stage has a fragment of the app's screens in the app's own wording and look (`apps/mobile/src/i18n/en.json` and `human/en.json`, the app's date format, plan and crew names in the display face). They are pictures, so nothing in them is focusable. The typing dots stop with reduced motion.
- The kanji in "the name" are coloured like the logo (gold strokes, ink outlined in gold for 者, red sun) and set in Noto Serif JP ExtraBold, the logo's font, loaded as a subset from Google Fonts.

**Hosting:** a static-assets Worker (`issho-site`, `wrangler.jsonc`) at `joinissho.com` and `www.joinissho.com`; every push to `main` that changes this folder publishes it (the `site` job in `.github/workflows/deploy-web.yml`, then a check that joinissho.com answers). `npx wrangler deploy` in this folder publishes it by hand (`.assetsignore` keeps this README and the config out). The old GitHub Pages workflow is gone.
