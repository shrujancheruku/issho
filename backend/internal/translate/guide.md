# all

Issho is a mobile app that helps people aged 18–40 make friends through small group outings of two to four hours (a photo walk, bouldering then lunch, a board-game café). The voice is warm, friendly and plain: a friend inviting you, not a company or a bank. Prefer what a native speaker would naturally write in an app over a word-for-word rendering, but keep the meaning exact, and don't add anything.

- **Issho** is the product's name. Always write it exactly as "Issho" in Latin letters; never translate or transliterate it.
- Keep every `{placeholder}` exactly as written (same braces, same name). Move it wherever the sentence needs it.
- Keep the source's punctuation style for the target language (full stops, question marks, the ellipsis "…").
- Buttons and labels: short, in the usual style of apps in that language (imperative verbs, or the noun the platform normally uses). Don't turn a button into a full sentence.
- Terms, used the same way everywhere:
  - **plan**: a suggested outing that people sign up for before it has a date. Not a schedule, a subscription or a project.
  - **outing**: the meetup itself, once it has a date and a group.
  - **crew**: a small, ongoing group chat of people who met at an outing and keep meeting.
  - **sign up**: say you'd like to go to a plan; it's not yet a commitment.
  - **check in**: say you've arrived at the meeting point.
  - **code of conduct**: the community rules people agree to.
  - **moderator**: a person on the Issho team who reviews reports.
- Address the reader in the informal, friendly second person where the language has one (see below), the way social apps for young adults do.

# ja

- Polite but friendly: です/ます for sentences; buttons and labels in the plain, short forms Japanese apps use (保存, 送信, ホーム, キャンセル).
- Write Issho as "Issho", never 一緒, 一笑 or いっしょ.
- Use katakana loanwords only where Japanese apps normally do; otherwise plain Japanese.
